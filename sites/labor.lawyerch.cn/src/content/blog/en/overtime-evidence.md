**The employee proves overtime first; if the employer holds the records and withholds them, the employer bears the consequence.**

## The rule

Article 42 of the Supreme People’s Court interpretation on labour disputes places the initial burden on the employee. Where the employee can show the employer holds evidence of overtime and the employer refuses to produce it, the employer bears the adverse consequence.

## Sequence

1. **Employee’s initial evidence**: clock-in screenshots, work-group messages, email timestamps, shift schedules, approval forms, witness statements.
2. **Employer-held evidence**: attendance systems, access logs, system activity.
3. **Non-production**: the tribunal may accept the employee’s account or draw an adverse inference.

## Practical notes

- **Tie evidence to dates and hours**: “often worked late” is not enough.
- **Preserve electronic evidence properly**: keep the original device and show identities and timestamps.
- **An approval policy is not a complete defence**: arranged or tolerated overtime can still count.
- **Limitation**: overtime pay is remuneration; claims during employment are not subject to the one-year limit.
