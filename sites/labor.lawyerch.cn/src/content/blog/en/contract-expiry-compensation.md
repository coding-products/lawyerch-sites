**In most cases yes — but not always.**

## The rule

Article 46(5) of the Labour Contract Law requires severance when a fixed-term contract expires, unless the employer offered to renew on the same or better terms and the employee refused.

Three questions decide the outcome: who proposed non-renewal, whether the renewal terms were maintained or improved, and whether a statutory exception applies.

## Common scenarios

| Scenario | Severance |
|---|---|
| Employer proposes non-renewal | Yes |
| Employer maintains or improves terms, employee refuses | No |
| Employer lowers terms, employee refuses | Yes |
| Employee proposes non-renewal | Generally no |
| Employee already drawing basic pension | No |

## Practical notes

- **Document the renewal offer**: position, pay, location and date, with signed acknowledgement.
- **Do not dress up a downgrade**: a pay cut followed by “voluntary” non-renewal is usually treated as employer-caused.
- **Calculation**: one month’s pay per full year of service; six months or more counts as a year, less than six months as half a month.
- **Cap**: where monthly pay exceeds three times the local average, the cap applies, with a maximum of twelve years.

> General information only, not legal advice for a specific case.
